# Next Meeting Prep Brief

Do not summarize everything known about the person. Prepare the owner to move the relationship or decision forward.

## Step 1 - Identify the meeting

Connected mode: check `~~calendar` for the requested meeting. For `next meeting`, prefer the next external meeting. Use internal meetings only when requested or no external meeting fits the stated window.

Standalone mode: accept the meeting title, attendees, and any pasted history.

If several meetings match, ask which one rather than guessing.

## Step 2 - Pull recent history

Default lookback: 90 days.

Check available sources:

- `~~meeting notes`: prior decisions, commitments, stated needs
- `~~CRM`: deal/account stage, value, next step, account owner
- `~~email`: last exchange, unanswered asks, promised follow-up
- `~~project tracker`: active delivery issues or dependencies
- Agency Brain: durable relationship context, decisions, constraints, preferences, and prior lessons

Keep older history only when it remains materially open or important.

## Step 3 - Reconcile sources

Prefer recent direct evidence over stale summaries.

When sources conflict, show the conflict. Do not silently merge `proposal approved` from an old note with `still reviewing` from a newer email.

Separate:

- fact
- open commitment
- inference
- recommendation

## Step 4 - Find open loops

Look specifically for:

- something the agency promised and has not completed
- something the other party promised and has not completed
- a decision that was deferred
- a proposal/estimate/renewal with no next step
- a project risk likely to come up
- a relationship concern or stated goal
- a previous question that never got answered

The brief should lead with open loops, not biography.

## Step 5 - Define one meeting outcome

Write one sentence:

`A good meeting ends with ______.`

Examples: approved next phase, agreed recovery plan, scheduled technical session, confirmed decision owner, clear no-go, or signed-off scope boundary.

Do not use vague outcomes such as `good conversation`.

## Output

Keep to one screen when possible.

```markdown
# Meeting Prep - [Name / Company]
**When:** [date/time]
**Meeting:** [title]
**Relationship:** [one sentence]

## Last time
[date] - [what was actually decided or left open]

## Open loops
- [who owes what / since when / impact]

## What they care about
- [stated goal/constraint with source]

## Watch for
- [risk, contradiction, or likely concern]

## 3 talking points
1. [specific]
2. [specific]
3. [specific]

## 5 questions
1. [question tied to a real gap]
2. ...

## Likely objections
- [objection] -> [grounded response]

## The ask
[one ask]

## What good looks like
[one outcome]

## Sources / confidence
[systems used + material gaps]
```

## Rules

**Do not invent objections.** If there is no evidence, label them as hypotheses.

**Do not reintroduce resolved issues.** Use current history.

**Do not hide an agency overdue commitment.** The owner should know before the other party raises it.

**Do not overload the owner with ten goals.** One ask and one desired outcome.

**Do not update CRM/tasks or send messages automatically.** Propose follow-up after the meeting and wait for approval.

**Use Agency Brain for durable context, not as a substitute for fresh operational data.** A current email or project status can supersede an older knowledge entry; flag the mismatch so the knowledge can be reviewed.

## What good looks like

The owner can walk into the meeting knowing what happened last time, what is still open, what to ask, and what outcome to leave with.
