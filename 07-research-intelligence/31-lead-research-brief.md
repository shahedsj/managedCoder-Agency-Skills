# Lead Research Brief

Outreach written on invented research is worse than outreach written on none. A single wrong detail in an opening line — the wrong role, the wrong company, a project they never worked on — tells the recipient the personalization is fake, and there is no recovering from that in the same conversation.

This skill produces a short, honest brief on a **person** before you write to them. [`04 Competitor & Prospect Scan`](04-competitor-prospect-scan.md) does the same job for their **company**. Use both before a big call.

> Tool placeholders like `~~CRM` mean whatever tool you've connected in that category. See [CONNECTORS.md](CONNECTORS.md).

## How it works

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  STANDALONE (always works)                                    │
│  ✓ Paste what you have: a profile, past emails, notes         │
│  ✓ Structured brief + an explicit list of what is unknown     │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  SUPERCHARGED (when you connect your tools)                   │
│  + ~~CRM: stored research, past activity, deal history        │
│  + ~~email: every prior exchange with this person             │
│  + ~~meeting notes: what they actually said on past calls     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## What I need from you

A name. Ideally a name plus a company, because names are not unique and researching the wrong person wastes the run and the call.

## Step 1 — Find the right person, and confirm it

Search your `~~CRM` for the name or company. If more than one person matches, **show the matches with title and company and ask which one.** Guessing here is the most expensive possible mistake, because everything downstream inherits it.

## Step 2 — Judge whether the stored research is still trustworthy

Most CRMs store research from whenever somebody last ran it. That date matters more than the content.

Refresh when **either** is true:

1. **It is more than 30 days old.** Roles, companies, and priorities change. Month-old research is background, not intelligence.
2. **You cannot tell what the research was actually based on.** If there is no record of what source material the research had access to, there is no way to separate findings from guesses. Age does not make that safe.

If a refresh takes under a minute, just do it and say you are refreshing. For more than three people at once, say how many runs that is and confirm first.

## Step 3 — Separate what is known from what is inferred

Split every finding into one of three buckets, and keep them separate in your head even if the output merges them:

| Bucket | Example | Safe to use in outreach? |
|---|---|---|
| **Stated** | Their title on their own profile. Something they said on a recorded call. | Yes |
| **Recorded** | A note a colleague wrote after a meeting two years ago. | Yes, with a hedge — "I think you mentioned..." |
| **Inferred** | "They probably care about X because they work in Y." | **No.** Use it to choose your angle, never as a fact in the email. |

The third bucket is where relationships get damaged. It is fine to *think* with inferences. It is not fine to *write* with them.

## Step 4 — Report it short

They are about to write an email or get on a call, not read a dossier.

```
[Name] — [title] at [company]
Research: [fresh, just run | X days old]
Profile: [url]

What we know:
- [finding]
- [finding]
- [finding]

Worth using in outreach:
- [the one or two things that give a real opening]

Not known:
- [the gaps that matter for this conversation]

Record: [link to the CRM record]
```

Take any link from the record itself. Never construct a URL from someone's name — it will often be wrong and 404.

## Step 5 — Say so when there is nothing

This is the part that matters most, and the part that gets skipped.

If the research comes back empty, say exactly that:

> The research ran but returned nothing on [name]. I'm not going to fill the gap with guesses. Their profile may be locked down, which is itself useful — it means email or a warm intro will work better than a cold DM.

**Never pad a thin result** with general knowledge about the company, assumptions from a job title, or a web search result presented as if it came from your CRM.

A locked-down profile is a real and useful finding. It tells you which channel to use. An invented detail tells you nothing and costs the deal.

## The rules that make this work

**Empty is fine. Wrong is not.** A brief that says "we know three things and here they are" is more useful than one that says twelve things, nine of which are guesses, with no marking of which is which.

**Research the person, not the company.** They are different jobs with different sources. Blending them produces a brief that says a lot about a market and nothing about who is picking up the phone.

**This skill does not write the outreach.** It hands the research back. Drafting is a separate step with separate rules — see [`29 Owner Email Rules`](../06-marketing-content/29-owner-email-rules.md) or [`16 LinkedIn DM Outreach`](../03-sales-pipeline/16-linkedin-dm-outreach.md).

**It does not edit the record either.** No follow-up date changes, no logged activity. Read-only means read-only.

## Worked example

**Input:** "Prep me for my call with Marcus at Halden Group tomorrow."

Two Marcuses matched. Asked which one — it was the newer record, and the older one was a different company entirely. That question alone saved the call.

Stored research was 94 days old and had no record of its sources. Refreshed it.

**Output:**

```
Marcus R — Operations Director at Halden Group
Research: fresh, just run
Profile: [url]

What we know:
- Moved into the Operations Director role 5 months ago (stated on profile)
- Halden acquired a smaller studio in March (company announcement)
- Posted twice about delivery process and capacity planning (stated)

Worth using in outreach:
- He is 5 months into a new role after an acquisition. That is exactly when
  delivery process gets rebuilt, and exactly when it hurts.

Not known:
- Whether he owns the vendor decision or influences it. Worth asking directly
  on the call rather than assuming from the title.
```

Notice the last block. "Not known" is doing real work — it stops the owner walking in and pitching a decision-maker who may not be one.

## Tips

1. **Do this before drafting, not while drafting.** Research done mid-sentence gets bent to fit the sentence.
2. **One specific true detail beats five general ones.** You only need one line that proves a human looked.
3. **A profile with nothing on it is a finding.** Some of the best prospects have no content at all. That means a different channel, not a worse lead.
4. **Check what your own team already knows first.** The most useful research is usually a note a colleague wrote after a call, not anything external.
5. **Note the date on everything.** "Recently promoted" from research 200 days old is not recent, and saying it out loud is a bad look.
