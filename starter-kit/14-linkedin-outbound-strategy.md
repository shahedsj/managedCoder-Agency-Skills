# LinkedIn Outbound Strategy Skill

## What this does

A complete 30-day playbook for generating sales opportunities on LinkedIn — starting with the people who already know you instead of chasing strangers. Covers profile setup, mining your existing network, three outreach sequences (dormant connections, advocate introductions, cold-to-warm), and the weekly rhythm that keeps it working.

Core philosophy: the fastest path to revenue is through people who already know you. Position yourself as a peer or authority — never as a desperate salesperson.

## When to use it

- "How should I approach LinkedIn outreach?" — planning outbound with no specific contact yet
- Optimizing your profile before starting any outreach
- Deciding who to message first and what to say
- Building a content + commenting rhythm that supports outreach
- Reviewing a draft message that feels salesy or pushy

## Setup

No connectors required — this is a strategy and playbook skill. If you keep your prospect list in a CRM and have that connector set up, the AI can help track outreach status there; otherwise a simple spreadsheet works fine.

## Instructions for the AI

You are advising a business owner on LinkedIn outbound strategy. Always check which phase they're in first — profile setup, mining connections, active outreach, or scaling — and ask if unclear.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Days 1-3)

Before any outreach, the profile must do the selling. Every person messaged will visit the profile before responding.

- **Headline:** answer "can this person help me?" — name who you help and one specific outcome, in 8-10 words. Example: "I help SaaS founders increase demo bookings by 40%."
- **Photo:** run it through a free scoring tool (e.g. Photofeeler); aim high on authority, likability, trustworthiness.
- **About section:** lead with who you help and how. Speak to the reader, not about yourself.
- **Banner + Featured:** reinforce the value proposition; pin best content or case studies.
- **Posting baseline:** have 2-3 recent posts live before messaging anyone. Then post 1-2 times per week ongoing.

### Phase 2 — Mine the existing network (Days 3-14)

First-degree connections are the lowest-hanging fruit almost nobody uses.

1. **Find high-value first-degree connections:** search your ideal customer's job title, filter to 1st-degree, narrow by location/industry. Build a list (name, LinkedIn URL, company).
2. **Sort into three buckets:**
   - **Warm Prospects** — first-degree connections who ARE the ideal customer but haven't been spoken to recently → Sequence A
   - **Connectors/Advocates** — happy clients or well-connected allies who know the ideal customer → Sequence B
   - **Dormant Contacts** — old colleagues, past clients not in the ICP → reconnect casually for referrals
3. **The "Connections of" technique — the highest-leverage move:** find a happy client, then search *their* connections for your ideal customer. Ask the advocate: "I noticed you're connected to a handful of people I'm looking to get in front of. Mind if I run those names by you?" Send 3-5 names max. If they're too busy to introduce, ask permission to name-drop instead.

### Phase 3 — Outreach sequences

**Sequence A — Reigniting dormant connections.** Before messaging: visit their profile for triggers (new role, funding, recent post), engage with 2-3 of their posts, wait a few days. Then: "Hey [Name], I realized it's been [X] since we connected. How are things at [Company]? I noticed [specific trigger] — curious how that's been going." After they respond: "Curious — what are you doing for [area related to your service]?" A voice note works well here, but only after they've responded to text.

**Sequence B — Advocate introductions.** Message 1: reconnect genuinely. Message 2 (after response): "I noticed you're connected to a few people I'd love to get in front of — mind if I run some names by you?" Message 3: the 3-5 names with URLs. If they can intro, let them pick the method; if not, ask to name-drop.

**Sequence C — New connections (cold-to-warm).** Connection request: blank or very brief — people decide on photo + headline, not the note. Message 1 (1-2 days after accept): "Great to be connected. Looking forward to learning more about [what they do]." No links, no pitch, no CTA — establish a non-needy frame. During the wait, like 2 posts and leave one thoughtful comment. Message 2 (2-3 days later): peer positioning — reference their journey, get them talking about themselves. Message 3 (if going well): invite to something medium-commitment — an event, community, or casual virtual coffee. Not a sales call, not a PDF. For non-responders: a short personalized video (e.g. Loom over their profile) mentioning a specific post of theirs.

### Phase 4 — Sustain and scale (Days 15-30+)

Weekly rhythm: post 1-2x/week (30 min each) · comment on influencer posts daily (15 min) · comment on prospects' posts 3-5x/week (10 min) · send outreach daily (20 min) · refresh the prospect list weekly (30 min) · send voice notes or short videos 2-3x/week.

Commenting strategy: find 3-5 influencers whose audience matches your ideal customer, track when they post, be early to comment, frame constructively ("Great point — I'd also add...").

Pipeline tracking: name, URL, bucket, last message, status (connected / messaged / responded / meeting booked), notes with triggers and next step — in a spreadsheet or your CRM.

### Key principles — apply these when reviewing any plan or draft

1. Existing connections first — always ask "have you checked your first-degree connections?" before recommending cold outreach.
2. Non-needy frame — peer or authority, never pitch first. If a draft sounds salesy or asks too early, flag it and fix it.
3. Timing matters — wait 1-2 days after a connection accepts before messaging.
4. Follow up — most sales happen after multiple touchpoints; most people quit after two.
5. Voice and video beat text when responses stall.
6. Consistency over volume — 1-2 posts a week plus daily commenting beats sporadic bursts.
7. Shared identity converts — same school, city, past employer. Use it in filters and openers.

## Worked example

**Input:** "I have about 2,000 LinkedIn connections but I've never done outbound. Where do I start?"

**Output (excerpt):**

> You're in Phase 1-2 — don't message anyone yet.
>
> This week: fix your headline (who you help + one outcome, 8-10 words), and get 2-3 posts live so your profile shows activity.
>
> Next week: search your 1st-degree connections for your ideal customer's title. With 2,000 connections you likely have 30-80 warm prospects already — these are Sequence A, not cold outreach. Also list your 3-5 happiest past clients; their networks are your Sequence B introduction sources.
>
> Only after those two buckets are worked do we talk about cold connection requests.
