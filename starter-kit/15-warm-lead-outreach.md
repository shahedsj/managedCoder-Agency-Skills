# Warm Lead Outreach

Your list is not cold. It is full of people who already met you, already liked you, and already forgot about you. The only thing that ruins that is a message that proves you forgot about them first.

> Tool placeholders like `~~CRM` mean whatever tool you've connected in that category. See [CONNECTORS.md](CONNECTORS.md).


Without connectors you lose the automatic history check, which is the most valuable part. This is the one skill genuinely worth connecting tools for.

## THE GOLDEN RULE: research first, draft second

**Never write a single word of outreach before completing full research on the contact AND getting explicit approval to proceed.**

This rule exists because past failures happened exactly this way. An email was drafted for someone the owner had met with multiple times, or spoken to just days before, or had a meeting scheduled the following week — and the draft treated them like a stranger.

That is worse than no outreach at all. It damages the relationship and embarrasses the person sending it.

The research runs in **parallel across 5 sources** and takes about **2 minutes**. Two minutes prevents every bad outcome on this page.

## Step 1 — Build the batch

Pull contacts past their next-follow-up date, or with no recent contact, ranked by whatever lead/priority score exists — or by deal value and staleness if there's no score. Present **10-15 candidates** as a shortlist. Ask which ones to work, and which channel fits each: email, LinkedIn, or both.

Keep a running list of who was already contacted in this session and recent ones. Don't repeat anyone on the same channel inside the cooldown window. Same person, two channels, same week reads as a campaign — which is exactly what you're trying not to look like.

## Step 2 — The pre-draft research checklist (every contact, no exceptions)

Run all 5 in parallel.

**1. ~~email — search four ways, not one.** Their address, their full name, their company name, and any alternate address you know. People change jobs and email domains; a single-address search returns "no history" for someone you emailed for two years. Read the most recent thread and note: date of last exchange, what was discussed, tone of the relationship, any open commitments. Also note internal notification threads — deal comments, mentions, assignment emails. These surface teammate activity before the deal record itself shows it.

**2. ~~calendar — search their name across past and future.** Look for: upcoming meetings in the next **2 weeks**, recent meetings in the last **30 days**, cancel/reschedule patterns, and recurring meetings.

**3. ~~CRM — lifecycle stage, last contact date, associated deals, notes.**

**4. ~~CRM deals — active deals, and critically whether any teammate has commented on or asked about the deal in the last 14 days.** Note deal name, stage, days in stage, next step, last activity.

**5. ~~LinkedIn profile — only when LinkedIn is the channel.** Spend **30-60 seconds** getting **one** sharp personalization hook: a post from the last **1-2 weeks**, a job change, or company news. Also note connection status (connected / pending / not connected) and, from any prior messages, their communication style — fast and casual, or slow and formal. **If there's no usable hook, say so plainly rather than inventing one.** A fabricated hook is detectable and it costs more than sending nothing.

## Step 3 — Present findings, then wait

Show this exactly. Do not skip to drafting.

```
--- [Contact Name] ([Company]) ---
Email: [most recent thread summary + date, or "No thread found"]
Calendar: [meetings found + dates, or "No meetings found"]
CRM: [lifecycle stage, last contact date, deal count]
Deals: [names and stages, or "None found"; note any team activity in last 14 days]
LinkedIn: [connection status; the one specific hook found, or "No URL / nothing usable"]

My read: [1-2 honest sentences on the relationship and what approach makes sense]

Draft it? Which channel — email, LinkedIn DM, or both? Any angle to focus on?
```

Then stop and wait. If the owner adds context — "we actually spoke last week about X" — that context beats everything the tools returned.

## Step 4 — When NOT to draft without asking first

Stop and ask in every one of these:

| Signal | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Upcoming meeting within **2 weeks** | "You have a meeting with [name] on [date]. Should I hold off?" |
| Email or DM thread within the last **7 days** | "You two were just in touch on [date]. What should this say given that?" |
| Active deal in **negotiation or proposal** stage | "There's an active deal at [stage]. Reference it, or keep this general?" |
| Active deal at **ANY stage** with team activity in the last **14 days** | "There's a deal at [stage] and [teammate] has been asking about it ([date]). Proceed, loop them in, or hold?" |
| A teammate is actively managing this lead | Flag it and suggest skipping, unless the owner wants to step in personally |
| Not yet connected on LinkedIn | A DM is impossible. Hand off for a connection request instead |

**On that fourth row — early stage is not automatically safe.** Team visibility is its own signal, independent of stage. This condition was added after a real dry-run test caught a contact with a "lead"-stage deal that a teammate was actively chasing for status. The old rule, which only stopped at negotiation and proposal, would have let it straight through.

## Step 5 — Set the relationship tier

This changes everything about the message, on either channel. Decide it before a single word gets written.

**TIER 1 — Knows you well.** Signals: 3 or more meetings or exchanges, ongoing relationship, active or past project, first-name basis in threads.

**Never use a cold introduction.** They know who you are. A cold intro to someone you've met multiple times makes the message look like it came from a bot with no memory of the relationship. It is one of the fastest ways to make a warm contact go cold. Open with something specific and real: *"Been a while since our last call."* *"Hope things are going well at [Company]."*

**TIER 2 — Has been in touch.** Signals: 1-2 meetings, a handful of emails or DMs, early-stage relationship, CRM shows "contacted." Open with a light callback: *"Wanted to follow up from our call a while back."*

**TIER 3 — Cold.** Signals: in the database, but no email thread, no calendar history, no CRM notes, not connected on LinkedIn. A brief one-line intro is fine here — and only here.

## Step 6 — Draft the email

```
To: [name] <[email]>
Subject: Re: [existing thread subject]   ← if a thread exists
         [short, specific, lowercase-ish] ← only if no thread exists
Thread: [thread ID — required when replying]
Format: HTML
Tier: [1 / 2 / 3]

Hey [First name],

[Opener matched to tier — real history, not a template line]

[Main message, 2-3 sentences max]

[One ask. One link if a call is the goal.]

[Sign-off]
```

**Reply into the existing thread by including the thread ID.** Not just quoting it — pass the actual thread ID so the draft lands as a reply in the conversation. Starting a fresh thread when one already exists is the single clearest signal that a message was automated.

**Never re-introduce yourself on an existing thread.** The last four messages are sitting right above yours.

**Set the content type to HTML.** This is the most common technical failure in the whole workflow. Without it the draft defaults to plain text, links appear as raw unclickable strings, and the email looks broken to the person receiving it. Required every single time, not just when there's a link.

**One clear ask.** Two asks get zero answers.

**Under 150 words.** Paragraphs of 2-3 sentences; one-sentence paragraphs are fine.

**Never open with "I hope this email finds you well"** or any variation of it.

**Drafts only. Never send.** The owner reviews and sends every message, without exception.

Run the draft through the Write in My Voice skill in this kit for tone and the banned-word check.

## Step 7 — LinkedIn channel: hand off

Once research is done (including item 5), the owner has approved, and the tier is set — hand off to the **LinkedIn DM Outreach skill** (file 16 in this kit) for everything about the actual message: the DM formula, connection request vs. DM, specificity rules, mirroring their pace, the closing line, voice-note guidance, and the send flow.

Pass it three things: the relationship tier, the personalization hook, and the connection status. Do not draft LinkedIn messages here. One source of truth per concern — two copies of the same drafting logic drift apart, and the older copy is the one that gets used.

## Step 8 — Enrichment (free sources first)

1. **Email signatures** — these often carry phone, title, and current company, and cost nothing
2. **~~CRM** — the record may already be richer than you think
3. **Any profile snapshots** you've already stored

Only after those, consider a paid enrichment tool. **Show the estimated cost and get approval BEFORE running it.** Enrichment credits get burned fast on contacts whose email signature already had the answer.

## Step 9 — Close out the session

```
OUTREACH SESSION — [date]
Drafted: [n]

1. [Name] — [Company] — [email / LinkedIn DM / connection request]
   [Subject line or hook used] | [reply in thread / fresh] | Tier [n]
2. ...

Skipped: [name] — [reason: meeting Thursday / teammate managing]

Ready to update the CRM: follow-up status → contacted, last contact
date → today, next follow-up → [today + 1 month]. Confirm? [waits]
```

Then, and only then, write the updates back: follow-up status, last contact date, and push the **next follow-up date to 1 month out** for everyone contacted.

**Never auto-write to any system. Confirm first, every time,** then report what actually changed — not what you intended to change.

## Worked example

**Input:** *"work my overdue follow ups, priya at nova retail is top of the list i think we talked recently"*

**Output:**

```
--- Priya Shah (Nova Retail) ---
Email: Last thread Aug 1 — you sent the checkout redesign proposal. No reply.
       Separate internal thread Aug 9: a teammate asked "any word from Nova?"
Calendar: No meetings found.
CRM: Stage "Proposal", last contact Aug 1, 1 open deal.
Deals: "Nova Retail — Checkout Redesign", Proposal, $15k.
       ⚠ Team activity 3 days ago.
LinkedIn: Connected. Posted 4 days ago about cart abandonment rates.

My read: Tier 1 — 4 meetings on record, first-name basis throughout. You
remembered talking recently, but the last real exchange was 11 days ago, not
this week. Proposal is sitting unanswered and her post is a clean, non-pushy
reason to resurface.

⚠ Before I draft: a teammate was chasing this deal 3 days ago. Proceed, loop
them in first, or hold?
```

The skill caught two things here. The owner's memory of "we talked recently" was 11 days stale — drafting from that memory produces a message with the wrong temperature. And the teammate activity would never have triggered a stop under a stage-only rule, because the deal is only at Proposal with no negotiation happening.

## Tips

1. **Two minutes of research is cheaper than one wrong email.** You cannot un-send a "great to e-meet you" to someone whose kids' names you know. There is no recovery message for that.
2. **The owner's memory is a source, not the source.** "I think we spoke recently" is wrong more often than the calendar is. Check it, don't argue with it — just show the date and let them correct the plan.
3. **If there's no honest hook, that's information.** A contact with nothing to say to them yet isn't ready for outreach. Move them down the list instead of manufacturing a reason.
4. **Reply-in-thread beats a clever subject line every time.** A reply lands in a conversation they already agreed to have. A fresh thread starts from zero and looks like a blast.
5. **Batch 10-15, work 3-5.** Ambition kills these sessions. Five researched, approved, well-tiered drafts beat forty generated ones you're too nervous to send.
