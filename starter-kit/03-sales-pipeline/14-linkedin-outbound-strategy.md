# LinkedIn Outbound Strategy

Most people burn their LinkedIn network chasing strangers while 2,000 people who already know their name sit untouched. This is the 30-day plan that works the warm list first — and the exact words to send.

## Core philosophy

Stop chasing strangers. The fastest path to revenue is through people who already know you. Position yourself as a peer or an authority — never as a desperate salesperson.

**The goal: generate sales opportunities in 30 days** by leveraging existing connections first, then expanding strategically.

**Option A — Starting cold.** "Help me build a 30-day LinkedIn plan." Tell me who your ideal customer is (title, industry, company size) and roughly how many connections you have.

**Option B — Mid-flight.** Tell me which phase you're in and what's stalling. I'll fix that phase, not restart you.

**Always diagnose the phase before advising.** If it's unclear, ask. The most expensive wrong guess is coaching outreach scripts to someone whose profile still doesn't answer "can this person help me?" — every message they send burns a contact permanently.

## Step 1 — Phase 1: Foundation (Days 1–3)

Before any outreach, the profile has to do the selling. Every person you message will visit your profile before they reply.

- **Headline.** It answers one question: "Can this person help me?" Name who you help and one specific outcome, in **8–10 words** — that's all people see in previews. Example: *"I help SaaS founders increase demo bookings by 40%."*
- **Profile photo.** Run it through a free scoring tool (e.g. Photofeeler). Aim for **8+ on authority, likability, and trustworthiness**. Take **50–60 phone shots** and pick the best natural expression. This is not vanity — it's the single thing people judge before they read a word.
- **About section.** Lead with who you help and how. Speak *to the reader*, not about yourself.
- **Banner image.** Reinforce the value proposition or credibility visually.
- **Featured section.** Pin your best content, case studies, or resources.

### Posting baseline

Have **2–3 recent posts live BEFORE any outreach**. People need to see you actually use the platform — an empty profile reads as a bot or a burner account.

- Post **3 times across the first few days** — not all on the same day.
- Topics: industry trends, customer insights, personal stories, lessons learned.
- Ongoing after launch: **1–2 posts per week**.

**Post formatting is not in this skill on purpose.** Use the Write in My Voice skill (file 12 in this kit) — it owns the hook, structure, and ending rules. Two skills giving contradictory advice on how to end a post is worse than one skill saying less.

## Step 2 — Phase 2: Mine the existing network (Days 3–14)

First-degree connections are the lowest-hanging fruit and almost nobody uses them.

### Find high-value first-degree connections

**LinkedIn free search:**
1. Type the ICP job title in the search bar (e.g. "CMO", "VP Sales")
2. Click **People** → **All Filters**
3. Select **1st** under connections
4. Narrow by location, company, industry, keywords
5. Build a list: name + LinkedIn URL + company

**Sales Navigator:**
1. Lead Filters → **1st degree connections**
2. Apply a persona, or fill in title, geography, industry, company size
3. Add: **"Posted on LinkedIn"** (they're active) and **"Changed jobs"** (they're open to conversation)

### Sort into three buckets

| Bucket | Who they are | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm Prospects** | 1st-degree connections who ARE the ICP but you haven't spoken to recently | Sequence A — reignite the relationship |
| **Connectors / Advocates** | Happy clients or well-connected allies who know the ICP | Sequence B — ask for introductions |
| **Dormant Contacts** | Old colleagues, event contacts, past clients not in the ICP | Casual reconnect for referrals |

### The "Connections Of" technique — the highest-leverage move

Find a happy client or strong contact, then search *their* connections for your ICP.

**LinkedIn free:** Search → All Filters → **"Connections of"** → type the advocate's name → add ICP filters (title, location, industry) → browse. These are 2nd-degree people the advocate can introduce.

**Sales Navigator:** Lead Filters → **"Connections of"** → advocate's name → apply persona/ICP filters → drill down with "Changed jobs" and "Posted on LinkedIn".

**Outreach to the advocate:**

```
Hey [Name], how's it going? By the way, I noticed you're connected to a
handful of people I'm looking to get in front of. Do you mind if I run
those names by you?
```

Send **3–5 names max**, each with LinkedIn URL + company. If they don't know those, send another batch. More than 5 at once turns a favor into homework and you get silence.

**If the advocate is too busy to introduce, ask permission to name-drop:**

```
Hey [Prospect], I was just talking with our mutual connection [Advocate
Name]. They mentioned you'd be great to connect with.
```

## Step 3 — Phase 3: The three outreach sequences

### Sequence A — Reigniting dormant first-degree connections

For people already in your network who match the ICP but you haven't spoken to recently.

**Before messaging:**
1. Visit their profile — look for triggers (new role, funding, recent post, company news)
2. If they post, engage with **2–3 posts first** (like + a thoughtful comment)
3. **Wait a few days.** Then message. Commenting and messaging on the same day reads as surveillance, not interest.

**Message:**

```
Hey [Name], I realized it's been [X months/years] since we connected.
How are things going at [Company]? I noticed [specific trigger].
Curious how that's been going.
```

**Follow-up, after they respond:**

```
That's great to hear. Curious — what are you guys doing for
[area related to your service]?
```

**Voice note:** send one only **AFTER they respond to text**. It's more personable and shows personality — but sent cold it's an ambush.

### Sequence B — Asking advocates for introductions

**Message 1 — Reconnect:**

```
Hey [Name], hope you're doing well! [Reference something genuine]
```

**Message 2 — The ask, after they respond:**

```
By the way, I noticed you're connected to a few people I'd love to get
in front of. Mind if I run some names by you?
```

**Message 3 — The names.** 3–5 names, each with LinkedIn URL and company.

If they can introduce → agree the method (email, LinkedIn, group chat). If they can't → ask permission to name-drop.

### Sequence C — New connections (cold-to-warm)

**Connection request:** leave it **blank or very brief**. People decide on your photo + headline, not the note. A pitch in the note is the fastest way to get ignored.

**Message 1 — wait 1–2 DAYS after they accept:**

```
Hi [Name], great to be connected. Looking forward to learning more
about [what they do].
```

**No links, no pitch, no CTA, no question.** This message exists to establish a non-needy frame. A question here forces a reply and puts you back in salesperson position.

**During the 1–2 day wait:** like 2 of their recent posts and leave a thoughtful comment on one.

**Message 2 — 2–3 DAYS later, peer positioning:**

```
Hey [Name], that must have been a big transition from [previous
role/company]. When I went through something similar, it was a lot.
How's it been going?
```

This acknowledges their journey, gets them talking about themselves, and signals shared experience — three things a pitch never does.

**Message 3 — the bridge, if the conversation is going well.** Invite them to something **medium-commitment**: a live event or webinar, a community or peer group, or a casual virtual coffee. **Not a sales call. Not a PDF.**

```
By the way, I run a [monthly roundtable / community / event] for
[role/industry] focused on [topic]. Would that be interesting to you?
```

**Loom video follow-up for non-responders:**
1. Open their LinkedIn profile
2. Start the Loom screen recording (they can see you're on their page — that's the point)
3. Record: *"Hi [Name], just wanted to say I really enjoyed your post about [X]. Looking forward to seeing more of your content."*
4. DM: *"Hey [Name], just made you a quick video."*

## Step 4 — Phase 4: Sustain and scale (Days 15–30+)

### Weekly rhythm — with the actual time budgets

| Activity | Frequency | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Post content | 1–2x per week | 30 min per post |
| Comment on influencer posts | Daily | 15 min |
| Comment on prospects' posts | 3–5x per week | 10 min |
| Send outreach messages | Daily | 20 min |
| Review & refresh prospect list | Weekly | 30 min |
| Send voice notes / Loom videos | 2–3x per week | 15 min |

That's roughly 45 minutes a day. Put it in the calendar or it won't happen — this phase is where nearly everyone quits, right before the first replies land.

### Commenting strategy

One of the fastest ways to get visibility, especially with a small following.

- Find **3–5 influencers** whose audience matches your ICP
- Track when they post (a simple sheet: profile URL + posting time)
- **Be first to comment** — the early comment gets seen by everyone who arrives later
- Frame constructively: *"Great point. I'd also add..."* — never direct contradiction. Arguing with an influencer gets you attention from the wrong 5%.

### Pipeline tracking

Track in a sheet or `~~CRM`: **Name · LinkedIn URL · Bucket (Warm / Connector / New) · Last message sent · Status (Connected / Messaged / Responded / Meeting booked) · Notes (triggers, shared context, next step)**

## The rules that make this work

1. **Existing connections first.** Before recommending any cold outreach, ask: *"Have you checked your first-degree connections first?"* Cold outreach converts a fraction of warm reconnects, and you only get one shot at each contact.
2. **Non-needy frame.** Peer or authority, never petitioner. Never pitch first, never ask upfront. The moment you need something from them, they stop being curious.
3. **Timing matters.** Wait 1–2 days after a connection is accepted. Green circle indicators help: **solid green = online now, hollow green = mobile notifications on**. Message when they'll actually see it.
4. **Follow up.** Most sales happen after multiple touchpoints. Most people quit after two. That gap is the whole opportunity.
5. **Voice and video beat text** once text stalls — they're harder to ignore and prove there's a person behind the account.
6. **Don't skip the profile.** Nothing downstream works if the profile doesn't answer "can this person help me?"
7. **Consistency over volume.** 1–2 posts a week plus daily commenting beats sporadic bursts, because prospects see you before AND after you message them.
8. **Shared identity converts.** Same school, city, past employer. Use it in filters and in openers — it moves you from stranger to almost-known in one line.

## When reviewing a plan or a draft

- **Check which phase they're in.** Ask if unclear.
- **Name the sequence.** Identify the bucket (Warm Prospect / Connector / New Connection) and use the matching sequence — don't invent a fourth one.
- **Give actual templates**, customized with their names, company, and trigger. Not "personalize the opener."
- **Flag anything salesy or too early.** If a draft pitches, links, or asks for time before a reply has happened, say so and rewrite it.
- **Check the weekly rhythm.** If they aren't posting or commenting, say plainly that outreach without content works far worse.
- **Suggest voice or Loom** when text has stalled.
- **Hand post copy to the Write in My Voice skill** (file 12) — that skill owns how a post is written.

## Tips

1. **If you've never posted, don't message anyone yet.** The reply rate difference between a live profile and a dead one is bigger than anything you'll gain from a cleverer script.
2. **Your happiest client is worth more than 500 cold connection requests.** Ten minutes running "Connections of" on their profile beats a week of prospecting, and almost nobody does it.
3. **The blank connection request feels wrong and works better.** Every instinct says explain yourself. Resist it — the note only gives them a reason to say no.
4. **Track replies, not sends.** Sends make you feel productive. If you sent 50 and got 1 reply, the problem is the profile or the frame — sending 100 next month just doubles the damage.
5. **Most people quit in week 3.** Warm reconnects reply in days; cold sequences take 2–3 weeks to produce a meeting. Week 3 is when it looks broken and is actually working.
